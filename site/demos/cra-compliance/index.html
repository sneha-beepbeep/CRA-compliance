CRA and AI Act Security Tracker

For vendors whose AI systems assess the creditworthiness of individuals in the EU. This walkthrough uses a fictional vendor; nothing you enter leaves your browser.

The compliance problem

A company that sells AI credit-scoring software into the EU has two sets of security rules to work through at once: the essential requirements in Annex I of the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) and the cybersecurity rule for high-risk AI in Article 15 of the AI Act.

On top of that, CRA Article 14 sets hard reporting clocks. Once the vendor becomes aware of an actively exploited vulnerability or a severe incident, an early warning is due within 24 hours and a notification within 72 hours.

In practice this work sits in spreadsheets and chat threads, and nobody can say at a glance which requirements have evidence, which AI risks are handled, or when the next report is due.

In the next steps you'll see the tracker's view of a fictional vendor, Northwind Scoring, then run a reporting drill yourself.

AI-risk register

Each AI-specific risk gets a handling stage, an owner, a "last tested" date and links to the requirements it relates to. There are no scores and no pass/fail.

      What this means for you

      Limits of this demo and the product.
      • The tracker is not a certification, conformity assessment, audit opinion or determination of compliance. It shows only what your team enters.
      • It does not file anything with any authority and sends no notifications. Someone has to look at the clock, including outside office hours.
      • Deadlines are only as right as the awareness time you enter.
      • The severe-incident "one month" rule (clamped to month end) is pending legal confirmation.
      • Requirement descriptions on this page were written for the demo; in the product, short titles are still pending legal review.
